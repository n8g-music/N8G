# N8G Character Bible

> *Three members. Three masks. Three positions. The characters are the vessel through which the music enters the world. They are never named. They are never unmasked. They are defined by what they carry — not who they are.*

---

## 0. The Rule of Three

N8G is three. The collective is not one person with two collaborators. It is not five with two guests. It is three — permanent, indivisible, balanced. The three positions on stage are fixed. Each member's mask is unique. Each member's beadwork is unique. Each member's colour assignment is unique. But no member is more important than another. The triangle has no lead point.

### Collective Identity

- **Names:** None. Members are identified in this document and all internal communication by their position: Left, Centre, Right. Never by initial. Never by pseudonym. Never by number.
- **Voices:** Each member's speaking voice may be heard in rare circumstances (live address in darkness, distorted interview audio) — but no voice is ever attributed to a specific member. The collective speaks. "We."
- **Biography:** No member's personal history, age, origin, training, or background is ever shared publicly or internally beyond what is strictly necessary for production. The mask erases biography.
- **Individuality:** Expressed through mask design, beadwork pattern, movement vocabulary, instrumental role — never through name, face, or personal story.

## 1. The Left Member

### Position
Stage left (audience's right when facing the stage). The dynamic pole. The outward-facing energy of the collective.

### Role
Primary instrumentalist. The Left Member handles the melodic and textural elements — keys, synthesis, sampling, live processing. The Left is the hand that shapes the sound in real time, responding to the Centre's foundation and the Right's structure.

### Mask Design — "The Horizon"

The Left Member's mask is distinguished by its horizontal emphasis — the copper plates are arranged in broad bands across the face, suggesting a landscape, a horizon line, the wide gesture of an open hand.

**Construction:**
- **Material:** Hammered copper, medium patina — older than the Centre's, younger than the Right's. Visible tool marks across the surface, especially along the brow band.
- **Plate arrangement:** Three horizontal bands across the full face:
  - **Brow band:** The widest plate — spans temple to temple, arches slightly with the brow bone. The upper edge is raw, uncut — the hammered edge of the copper sheet left exposed. A single row of beadwork frames the upper border.
  - **Centre band:** Narrower — spans from below the eyes to the upper lip. Two symmetrical cutouts for vision — horizontal slits, not round holes. The slits are 3mm high, 30mm wide. No eye is visible through them in any lighting condition.
  - **Jaw band:** The deepest plate — covers the jaw, wraps slightly under the chin. The lower edge follows the jawline. Two vertical indentations at the chin suggest speech without revealing it.
- **Surface treatment:** The brow band carries three scored horizontal lines, shallow, 2mm apart — the mark of the number 3. The centre band is smooth except for the vision slits. The jaw band carries the heaviest patina — darkest at the chin, lightening toward the edges.
- **Fastening:** Visible copper rivets at the temples (2 each side) connecting the brow and centre bands. The jaw band is attached by leather strapping at the jaw hinge — visible, functional.
- **Weight:** The heaviest of the three masks. The Left carries weight.

### Beadwork — "The Horizontal Field"

The Left Member's beadwork is the most extensive of the three — a collar and cuff set that frames the instrumental work.

**Pattern:** Horizontal bands of seed beads in alternating widths — wide (12mm), narrow (4mm), wide (12mm) — repeating. The wide bands carry geometric patterns in the accent colour; the narrow bands are solid charcoal.

**Placement:**
- Collar: A standing collar, 40mm high, encircling the neck. Visible above the clothing, below the jaw band of the mask.
- Cuffs: Both wrists, 25mm wide. The beadwork extends to the knuckle on the dorsal side, shorter on the palmar side to allow full hand freedom.

**Colour assignment:** The Left Member's accent is **Clay** (`#A0522D`) — the warm earth tone that connects the collective to the ground, to the soil of the Highveld, to the source.

### Clothing Language

**Silhouette:** Broad-shouldered, structured. The Left Member's clothing creates width — a horizontal counterweight to the Centre's verticality.

**Materials:**
- Raw linen, unbleached, heavy weight — the primary fabric
- Vegetable-tanned leather at the shoulders and cuffs — dark brown, matte
- Copper thread in subtle embroidery along the collar edge and cuff borders

**Garments:**
- An oversized work coat — no collar (the beadwork collar serves this function), dropped shoulders, raw hem. The coat is unlined; the interior seams are visible.
- Wide-leg trousers in the same raw linen, cropped slightly above the ankle.
- Bare feet or leather sandals — never shoes. The Left is grounded.

**Evolution Rules:**
- The coat silhouette can shift: longer, shorter, fuller, more tailored — but always broad-shouldered, always raw linen.
- Embroidery patterns can evolve per release — always geometric, always copper thread, always subtle.
- Beadwork patterns change per release within the horizontal band structure.
- **Never changes:** The horizontal emphasis. Raw material language. Bare feet or sandals.

### Movement Vocabulary

The Left Member moves in wide gestures — arms open, expansive. Playing style is visible, physical, demonstrative. The hands are always in motion when sound is present. Between pieces, the Left becomes still — arms at sides, head slightly bowed — a deliberate contrast.

## 2. The Centre Member

### Position
Centre stage. The anchor. The gravitational centre around which Left and Right orbit.

### Role
The voice and the foundation. The Centre Member is the primary vocal presence — not a "singer" in the traditional sense, but the source of vocal texture, spoken word, tonal drone, the human breath made audible. The Centre also provides the low-end foundation: bass, sub-bass, the frequencies felt more than heard.

### Mask Design — "The Vertical"

The Centre Member's mask is the most architectural — vertical emphasis, rising from the face like a column, the most imposing of the three. It is the mask that photographs in silhouette as a single unbroken form.

**Construction:**
- **Material:** Hammered copper, light patina — the newest mask, the brightest copper. The surface is the most reflective of the three, catching and holding light differently at every angle.
- **Plate arrangement:** Three vertical plates, fused at the centreline:
  - **Centre column:** A single unbroken plate from hairline to chin, 50mm wide at the forehead, tapering to 35mm at the chin. The vertical ridge is pronounced — a raised spine down the exact centre of the face. This is the defining feature of the Centre's mask.
  - **Left face plate:** Covers the left half of the face, meeting the centre column at a visible seam. The seam is held by 5 copper rivets, evenly spaced, polished brighter than the surrounding surface.
  - **Right face plate:** Mirrors the left. The same rivet pattern. Perfect symmetry.
- **Vision:** Two vertical slits — 4mm wide, 35mm tall — positioned on either side of the centre column. They follow the natural eye position but extend above and below, elongating the apparent face.
- **Mouth:** No opening. The Centre's mask covers the mouth completely — the voice emanates from behind solid copper. This is intentional: the sound comes from the whole body, not a moving mouth.
- **Surface treatment:** The raised centre spine is burnished to a higher shine than the surrounding plates. In stage light, it reads as a single bright vertical line — the axis of the collective.
- **Fastening:** Hidden — the mask is secured at the back of the head with leather strapping that is never visible from the front or sides. The mask appears to float, held by its own weight and presence.

### Beadwork — "The Vertical Descent"

The Centre Member's beadwork is the most restrained — a single line that runs from the mask's jaw edge down the centre of the chest, disappearing into the clothing.

**Pattern:** A single strand of seed beads, 8mm wide, woven in a repeating chevron pattern. The chevrons point downward — a visual arrow toward the heart, toward the ground, toward the foundation.

**Placement:**
- A single vertical band from the chin of the mask to the solar plexus — approximately 250mm in length.
- The band is attached to the mask at the jaw edge and to the garment beneath at the collar — it bridges mask and clothing, never floating free.
- No cuff beadwork. The Centre's hands are bare of beadwork. The focus is drawn upward to the face, downward to the centre.

**Colour assignment:** The Centre Member's accent is **Burnished Copper** (`#D4956A`) — the brightest, rarest copper tone. The Centre is the light that the Left and Right play within.

### Clothing Language

**Silhouette:** Narrow, vertical, elongated. The Centre's clothing draws the eye upward — a column of fabric that echoes the vertical mask.

**Materials:**
- Lightweight wool crepe, charcoal — the primary fabric. It drapes; it does not structure.
- Raw silk at the collar and cuffs — natural, undyed, with a slight lustre that catches light.
- No leather. No embroidery. The Centre's clothing is the simplest of the three — the mask carries the visual weight.

**Garments:**
- A floor-length tunic — narrow cut, high collar (the beadwork band sits on top of it), full-length sleeves that taper to the wrist. The tunic falls straight from the shoulders to the floor.
- Narrow trousers in the same wool crepe, invisible beneath the tunic.
- Bare feet, always. The Centre is rooted.

**Evolution Rules:**
- The tunic length is fixed — floor-length, always. The cut may evolve: wider, narrower, sleeveless, full-sleeve — but always vertical, always floor-length.
- Fabric weight may shift between seasons — heavier wool for winter, lighter crepe for summer — but the charcoal colour is fixed.
- Beadwork chevron pattern evolves per release within the single-vertical-band structure.
- **Never changes:** Vertical silhouette. Charcoal wool. Floor-length tunic. Bare feet.

### Movement Vocabulary

The Centre moves least of the three. When the Centre moves, everyone notices. Movement is slow, deliberate, vertical — rising from stillness, returning to stillness. The hands stay close to the body. The head tilts rather than turns. The Centre's stillness is the most powerful visual on the stage — it commands attention through economy.

## 3. The Right Member

### Position
Stage right (audience's left when facing the stage). The structural pole. The architect of the collective's internal geometry.

### Role
Rhythm keeper and spatial designer. The Right Member handles percussion — both acoustic and electronic — and the spatial elements of the production: panning, depth, the placement of sound within the stereo field. The Right is the grid, the clock, the skeleton that the Left and Centre dress with melody and voice.

### Mask Design — "The Faceted"

The Right Member's mask is the most complex — angular, crystalline, built from smaller plates that catch light from multiple directions simultaneously. It is the mask that changes most dramatically under different lighting conditions.

**Construction:**
- **Material:** Hammered copper, heavy patina — the oldest mask, dark with age. Deep browns and near-charcoal tones dominate, with bright copper visible only in the recesses where oxidation has been slowed.
- **Plate arrangement:** The mask is constructed from 8 to 12 individual plates, each a different geometric shape — trapezoids, parallelograms, irregular quadrilaterals. The plates fit together like a puzzle, each seam a deliberate angle.
  - The plates are smaller than those of the Left and Centre — the mask reads as faceted, like cut stone or crystal.
  - The seams are pronounced — each plate edge is raised slightly, creating a grid of shadow lines across the face.
  - No plate is the same size or shape as any other. The asymmetry is intentional.
- **Vision:** Multiple small perforations — a scatter pattern of round holes, 2mm diameter each, distributed across the plates that cover the eye area. The perforations form no pattern — they are random, organic, like stars in a constellation.
- **Surface treatment:** Each plate carries a different surface texture — some smooth, some cross-hatched, some stippled, some left raw from the hammer. The variety creates visual complexity at close range and a unified dark presence at distance.
- **Fastening:** The plates are joined by copper wire loops — visible, functional, almost industrial. The mask reads as assembled, constructed, designed — never organic, never mask-like in the traditional sense.

### Beadwork — "The Geometric Grid"

The Right Member's beadwork is the most intricate — a grid pattern that extends from the mask border across the shoulders and down both arms.

**Pattern:** A diagonal grid of seed beads — lines crossing at 45° and 135° — forming a repeating diamond field. Each diamond cell is 15mm wide, 20mm tall. Within every third diamond, a single bead of the accent colour — like a point on a map, a node in a network.

**Placement:**
- Mask border: A 10mm band around the entire perimeter of the mask where it meets the skin, visible as a frame.
- Shoulder mantle: A capelet structure — beadwork on a stiffened fabric base — covering the shoulders and upper arms. Width from collar to mid-bicep. The mantle is the Right Member's most distinctive visual feature.
- Wrist: A single narrow band on the dominant wrist (right wrist, audience's right) — 12mm wide, the same diagonal pattern.

**Colour assignment:** The Right Member's accent is **Charcoal** (`#1A1A1A`) — the darkest tone in the palette. The Right is the structure, the night, the grid that holds the collective together. Charcoal beadwork on dark copper mask is subtle at a distance — a detail for those who look closely.

### Clothing Language

**Silhouette:** Angular, structured, architectural. The Right Member's clothing creates geometric shapes — sharp shoulders, straight lines, defined edges.

**Materials:**
- Waxed cotton — charcoal, with a slight sheen — the primary fabric. It holds shape. It catches light at the edges.
- Brushed metal hardware — copper, matte — at closures and structural points.
- No linen, no wool, no silk — the Right is the counterpoint to the organic materials of Left and Centre.

**Garments:**
- A structured jacket — sharp shoulders, stand collar (the beadwork mantle sits over and around it), asymmetrical closure (single copper fastening at the left shoulder). The jacket is cropped at the hip.
- Tapered trousers in the same waxed cotton, narrow at the ankle, with a copper zip at the outer ankle seam.
- Bare feet — but the Right may wear a single copper anklet, thin, unadorned. The only jewellery permitted on any member.

**Evolution Rules:**
- The jacket's geometry can evolve — sharper, softer, longer, shorter — but always structured, always waxed cotton, always charcoal.
- The beadwork mantle pattern evolves per release within the diagonal grid structure.
- The copper anklet is optional per performance — but if worn, it must be plain.
- **Never changes:** Angular silhouette. Waxed cotton. Charcoal. Beadwork mantle.

### Movement Vocabulary

The Right Member moves with precision — clean angles, no wasted gesture. Percussive playing style: sharp attacks, clear releases. The hands move independently — left and right often in different patterns, different rhythms. Between pieces, the Right stands with hands clasped behind the back — architectural, still, observing.

## 4. Stage Configuration

### The Triangle

The three positions form an equilateral triangle — each member equidistant from the others. The distance between members is 3 to 4 metres, depending on stage size. The Centre is forward of Left and Right by approximately 1 metre — the triangle points toward the audience, but the Centre is the apex, not the point of a pyramid.

```
            AUDIENCE
               |
               |
          [Centre]
          /       \
         /         \
     [Left]------[Right]
```

This formation never changes. Even on the largest stages, the three remain within a 4-metre triangle. The intimacy of their proximity is the point — the audience witnesses a private conversation made public.

### Instrument Placement

- **Left Member's instruments:** Arranged in a horizontal arc — keyboards, controllers, samplers spread across the width of the position. The Left moves along this arc during performance.
- **Centre Member's instruments:** A single station — a low table at waist height. Minimal: one primary instrument (bass or synthesiser), microphone stand (copper, gooseneck, no pop filter — the breath is part of the sound), and possibly a single effect unit. Nothing else.
- **Right Member's instruments:** A vertical arrangement — percussion mounted at multiple heights, electronic pads on stands, spatial controllers. The Right reaches upward as often as outward.

### Lighting Configuration

Three pools of warm light (3000K), one centred on each member's position. The pools overlap slightly — the edges blur into each other. The space between members is lit by the overlap, not by a separate source. The triangle of light on the floor is itself a symbol.

The audience is in darkness. No house lights. No audience illumination. The fourth member is present in the dark — felt, not seen.

## 5. Mask Rules (Universal)

These rules apply to all three masks, all three members, in all contexts:

### Never Removed
The mask is never removed on camera, on stage, in any photography, in any video, in any public or semi-public context. Backstage, in private rehearsals, in the studio — mask discipline is maintained. The mask becomes the face. There is no "behind the mask" content.

### Patina Evolution
Masks oxidise over time. Copper darkens. This evolution is documented, not prevented. Each mask's patina is part of its character. The Right Member's mask is deliberately the darkest; the Centre Member's is deliberately the brightest. As they naturally evolve toward equilibrium, they may be selectively polished or left to age — but never artificially patinated for effect.

### Mask Photography Rules
- The mask is photographed as object — sculptural, architectural, worthy of study.
- Never photograph the mask as "costume" or "accessory."
- Detail shots (rivets, patina, surface texture) are encouraged.
- The mask should never appear humorous, casual, or incidental. It is always treated with gravity.
- No mask is photographed without its member — the mask is not a prop. It is not displayed empty.

## 6. Beadwork Rules (Universal)

### Material Integrity
All beadwork uses glass seed beads sourced from South African artisans. Size: 11/0 (approximately 2mm diameter). The beads are matte finish — never gloss. The sound of beadwork (the soft click of beads against each other when members move) is part of the N8G sonic texture and is never edited out of live recordings.

### Evolution Protocol
- Beadwork patterns evolve per release (album, EP, major single).
- The new pattern is designed before the release's visual identity is finalised — beadwork influences album art, not the reverse.
- Pattern evolution follows the individual member's structural rules (horizontal for Left, vertical for Centre, diagonal for Right).
- Old patterns are archived, not discarded. The beadwork archive is a physical and photographic record of the collective's visual history.

### Repair and Maintenance
Beadwork is maintained. Broken strands are repaired. Faded beads are replaced. The beadwork must look lived-in (not pristine, not neglected). The maintenance is visible — new beads among old, slightly different tones — and that visibility is intentional. The collective ages visibly, honestly.

## 7. Clothing Evolution Rules

### What Can Change (Per Release)
- Silhouette details: width, length, proportion — within each member's structural rules
- Fabric weight: seasonal variation permitted
- Beadwork pattern: per Evolution Protocol
- Embroidery and surface detail: added or removed per release
- The Copper Anklet (Right Member only): worn or not worn

### What Never Changes
- Each member's structural silhouette (broad/horizontal, narrow/vertical, angular/structured)
- Each member's primary fabric (raw linen, wool crepe, waxed cotton)
- Each member's colour assignment
- Each member's beadwork placement zones
- Bare feet (all members, always)
- No names, no logos, no visible branding on any garment

### Documentation
Every release's clothing configuration is photographed and archived. The clothing archive is an internal resource — never published as "fashion" content, never styled for editorial, never shared as behind-the-scenes. The clothing is not a story. It is a fact of the world the release creates.

## 8. Internal Communication

### Referring to Members
- **In creative direction:** "Left," "Centre," "Right"
- **In production notes:** "L," "C," "R"
- **In technical documentation:** "Position 1," "Position 2," "Position 3"
- **Never:** Any given name. Any pseudonym. Any initial beyond L/C/R. Any biographical reference.

### Collective Voice
All public communication uses "we." Internal communication may distinguish positions for clarity — "Centre's vocal take," "Left's synth patch," "Right's percussion pattern" — but never for credit, attribution, or identity.

### Guests and Collaborators
Rare, but permitted. A guest artist joins the triangle — never replacing a member, never standing in a member's position. The guest stands at a fourth point, behind and between Left and Right, forming a diamond with the audience as the fifth point. The guest is unmasked — they are the visitor, the acknowledged other. Their name may appear in credits. Their face may be seen.
